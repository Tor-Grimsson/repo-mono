## Voice & Tone Guidelines

### Core Principles

Kolkrabbi's voice is **factual, grounded, and direct**. Copy should describe what something *is*, not why someone should want it. This means avoiding promotional adjectives, sensationalized phrases, and sales-oriented language. The tone focuses on clarity and precision over persuasion.

Write in **third-person perspective** exclusively (except in form fields where second-person is logical, such as "Your mail address"). Remove first-person plural pronouns ("we," "our") and second-person pronouns ("you," "your") from all headings, descriptions, and CTAs. This maintains an objective stance that lets the work speak for itself.

**Avoid promotional language**: Remove subjective adjectives like "comprehensive," "distinctive," "elegant," "curated," "versatile," or "strategic." These words add marketing spin without adding information. Instead, use specific, technical descriptors that explain functionality, purpose, or characteristics. For example, "Variable serif typeface for editorial design" over "An elegant contemporary typeface for versatile editorial systems."

### Writing Patterns

**Be specific and technical**: Use precise terminology that describes what something does or what it is built for. "3-axis variable sans with weight and width control" is better than "flexible modern typeface." Technical precision demonstrates expertise and helps users understand exactly what they're looking at.

**Keep it direct**: Get to the point without embellishment. "Typeface library with specifications and previews" is clearer and more useful than "Browse the complete collection of distinctive typefaces." Short, factual statements respect the reader's time and intelligence.

**Focus on structure and function**: Emphasize the systematic, foundational aspects of the work. Kolkrabbi is about structure, clarity, consistency, and long-term adaptability—not superficial aesthetics or trends. Copy should reflect this approach by describing how systems work, what problems they solve, and what capabilities they provide.

### Reference Example: Studio Page Approach Section
k
The Studio Page Approach section exemplifies the desired tone perfectly:

> "At its core, Kolkrabbi is systematic, not superficial. From typography and color logic to layout, interaction, and asset management — each step serve clarity, consistency, and long-term adaptability. The studio works with clients of all sizes, particularly those ready to refine, evolve, or rethink their visual presence."

This copy is factual (describes actual approach), grounded (no hyperbole), third-person (no "we" or "our"), and direct (states position clearly). It focuses on what the studio does and how it works, not on persuading readers why they should choose it.

### What to Avoid

- **Pronouns**: "Subscribe to our newsletter" → "Subscribe to the newsletter"
- **Promotional adjectives**: "Comprehensive documentation" → "Documentation covering design tokens, patterns, and implementation guidelines"
- **Sales phrases**: "Discover our curated showcase" → "Collections of illustrations, logomarks, and visual experiments"
- **Vague benefits**: "delivered to your inbox" → Simply describe what updates include
- **Over-promising**: "establish distinctive market presence" → "visual systems for brand identity"

### Action Verbs & CTAs

Use clear, active verbs that describe the action without overselling: "Browse," "Explore," "View," "Launch," "Get," "Subscribe," "Download." Avoid marketing-speak like "Discover," "Unlock," "Transform," or "Elevate."
