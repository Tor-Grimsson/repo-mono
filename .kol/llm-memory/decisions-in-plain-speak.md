When asking the user to decide something, write each point as a plain sentence a non-reader-of-the-plan understands: name the thing in page terms ("the logo link top-left", "the light/dark switch"), say what I'll do, ask the yes/no. Compressed telegraph style ("tooltip skips (wordmarks/prototype/thumbs) · dead STATIC_META") is unreadable to him and triggers real frustration.

**Why:** 2026-07-28: "I AM A HUMAN, I NEED HUMAN SPEAK" after a slug-list of audit correction points. Terse ≠ compressed: the standing "minimal words" ruling means no padding, not jargon density.

**How to apply:** Reports stay tight tables/checklists, but any line that asks him to choose gets spelled out in full plain language, one decision per line/paragraph. See [[checkpoint-question-blocks-execution]].
